# Fact check, 3 November 2026

## How it was done

All 100 paintings were reviewed: every true statement, every false statement (to make sure none is accidentally true), and every summary. Claims that were uncertain were checked against museum pages and other published sources. Everything else was checked against established art-historical knowledge, not against a source for each line, so a museum-by-museum check is still the gold standard if you want one.

## Checked and confirmed

- Rembrandt, Self Portrait at 63: X-rays show he first held a brush (and maulstick) ([National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/stories/rembrandt-in-detail), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Portrait_at_the_Age_of_63))
- Rachel Ruysch's paintings sold for more than Rembrandt's had ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Ruysch))
- Hammershøi painted about 60 interiors at Strandgade 30 (sources say about 60 to 66)
- The Art Institute of Chicago owns six of Monet's Stacks of Wheat
- The Kiss was bought by the Austrian state in 1908 while still unfinished ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kiss_(Klimt)))
- Clara Peeters signed her name on the knife handle ([Mauritshuis](https://tour.mauritshuis.nl/-clara-peeters/still-life-with-cheeses,-almonds-and-pretzels/en_GB))

## Worth a second look if you want to be thorough

These are widely repeated but rest on secondary sources:

- Degas: dancers make up "more than half" of his work
- Leyster's paintings were credited to Frans Hals "for about two centuries"
- The Laughing Cavalier's title dates from an 1888 exhibition
- Rousseau studied plants at the Jardin des Plantes (he also used books and illustrations)
